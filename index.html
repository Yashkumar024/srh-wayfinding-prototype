Campus Berlin Wayfinding

A UX design prototype for an SRH University Berlin entrance kiosk that hands a route off to the student’s phone browser with a QR code. It includes low-fidelity wireframes, matching high-fidelity screens and a click-through mockup.

The kiosk app shows real QR codes: scan one with your phone camera and the route opens in your phone’s browser. There is no GPS, no backend and no route calculation, and every route is an illustrative placeholder.

User flow

The kiosk stands just outside the university entrance. Students choose one of four destinations, scan the QR code and follow the route on their phone one step at a time.

Click-through

Tap a destination on the kiosk, then use “Simulate phone scan” to stand in for the camera. The buttons only switch between mockup screens. Nothing is tracked or calculated.

Prototype control only. On a real kiosk, the student scans the QR code with their own phone camera.

Accessibility & placeholders

Design decisions and open items to check before this moves to build.

Must be validated on site

The final kiosk mounting height and the reachable touch area must be checked on site with wheelchair users. The seated-reach zone shown here is a design intent (roughly the lower half of the screen), not a measured specification.

Kiosk reach & touch

  • All essential controls (the 4 destinations, Back, Help and EN/DE) sit in the lower seated-reach zone.
  • The title, illustration and map preview are placed higher because they are read-only.
  • Buttons are full width and 56–58 px tall with 10 px gaps. Every button has a text label, never an icon alone.
  • The QR code sits at mid-height so it can be scanned standing or seated.

Readability

  • Charcoal on warm white for body text. Charcoal text on orange buttons, for higher contrast than white on orange.
  • Large type: 46 px kiosk title, 21 px buttons, 30 px phone instructions.
  • One instruction per phone screen, written in plain language.
  • English is the default, with German available on the kiosk.

Placeholder content

  • Maps are schematic blocks and corridors, labelled “Illustrative route · not to scale”.
  • Step texts, times and distances are invented examples, including the lift and stair wording.
  • The QR codes are real: each opens route.html#<destination> in the phone’s browser. They are generated by tools/make-qr.swift.
  • All route content lives in routes.js, so it can be replaced once verified directions exist.
